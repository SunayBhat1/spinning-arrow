Ethan Frome lives with his wife, Zeena, on an isolated and financially troubled farm near Starkfield, Massachusetts. He once studied away from home, but family illness and responsibilities interrupted that possibility. He now works the farm and a small mill. Zeena came to help during his mother's illness and remained as his wife. Their household is quiet, marked by money problems, difficult work, and Zeena's concerns about her health. Ethan has few occasions to discuss his interests or the life he once imagined.

Mattie Silver, a young relative of Zeena's, has come to help with the housework. She has little money and limited prospects after family misfortunes. Her presence changes Ethan's experience of the house. They share observations and small moments of companionship, and Ethan looks forward to walking home with her after village gatherings. Mattie is not consistently effective at household tasks, which becomes a source of complaint for Zeena. Ethan sometimes quietly helps with work to prevent criticism. His attachment grows while he remains uncertain about what Mattie feels.

One winter evening, Ethan watches Mattie at a village dance and walks home with her. They pass a steep sledding hill and discuss taking a ride together. He is conscious of another young man's interest in Mattie and feels the possibility that she might have a life away from him. Their walk and conversation make his own feelings more difficult to ignore. At home, ordinary interactions with Zeena return him to the constraints of their household. No shared plan for leaving has been made, and their attachment is largely expressed indirectly.

When Zeena goes away for a medical consultation, Ethan and Mattie spend an evening alone in the house. Mattie uses a red glass dish that Zeena treasures. A cat breaks it, and they try to conceal the accident until Ethan can repair it. The evening contains familiar domestic tasks, conversation, and the awareness of Zeena's absence. Ethan imagines a life in which such companionship could be ordinary, but he and Mattie do not resolve how to obtain it. The dish remains broken, and Zeena's return brings the matter into the open.

Zeena says her health requires more help and announces that she has arranged for a hired girl to replace Mattie. Mattie is to leave the next day. Ethan disputes the expense and objects to sending her away, but Zeena persists. When the broken dish is discovered, she accuses Mattie of using something she should not have touched. Ethan and Mattie confront the impending separation. Ethan thinks about escaping with her and starts to imagine what that would require. The farm's debts and his lack of available money obstruct the plan.

Ethan considers asking Andrew Hale for an advance. He anticipates presenting his household difficulties in a way that might obtain sympathy and money. An encounter with Mrs. Hale, who expresses concern about his burdens and speaks warmly of his care for Zeena, makes him abandon that approach. He returns without the funds that might have enabled a departure. When the time comes, he insists on driving Mattie toward the station himself. They take a longer route, revisit places associated with happier moments, and talk more openly about what they mean to each other.

Near the sledding hill they find a sled and take a successful run. The elm at the bottom is a known obstacle; Ethan has already demonstrated that he can steer around it. They remain together as the time for Mattie's train approaches. Ethan asks where she would go if she caught it, and neither has a reassuring answer. Mattie then asks him to take her down again so that they will never come back up. When he asks what she means, she tells him to go directly into the big elm so that they will never have to leave each other again.
