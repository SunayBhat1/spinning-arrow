# Batch A: 25 public-domain short stories (English/American)

All published before 1929. Gutenberg ebook pages were checked live on 2026-10-11 (collection volumes where noted).

## A01. Young Goodman Brown — Nathaniel Hawthorne (1835)
- ~5000 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/512
- **Plot:** A young Puritan leaves his new wife Faith for one night to keep an appointment in the forest, where he finds his town's most pious people gathered for a witches' sabbath.
- **Focal:** Goodman Brown
- **Stop:** At the forest altar, the devil-figure calls him and Faith forward for baptism. Stop before he cries out.
- **Question:** "I am standing at the altar beside Faith, about to be initiated. Everyone I revered is here. What do I do?"
  - A. Accept the baptism with Faith, accepting that everyone is sinful
  - B. Cry out to Faith to look to heaven and resist, then go home and live among these people as before, forgiving them
  - C. Resist, then publicly expose and denounce the townsfolk I saw
  - D. Resist, then quietly cut myself off from everyone, Faith included, trusting no one
- **Author's path:** He cries 'Faith! Look up to heaven,' wakes alone, and lives the rest of his life as a gloomy, distrustful man who shuns his wife and his neighbors.
- **Axis:** Moral purity vs charity and community

## A02. Rappaccini's Daughter — Nathaniel Hawthorne (1844)
- ~10500 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/512
- **Plot:** In Padua a student, Giovanni, falls for Beatrice, whose scientist father has made her poisonous by raising her among toxic plants. Giovanni finds that he has become poisonous too.
- **Focal:** Giovanni Guasconti
- **Stop:** Baglioni has given Giovanni an antidote, and Giovanni has just found that his own breath kills insects. He is going to meet Beatrice in the garden. Stop before he speaks to her.
- **Question:** "I have become poisonous because of her. I hold an antidote that may kill or cure her. What do I say and do when I meet Beatrice?"
  - A. Give her the antidote gently, telling her it is a risk we share
  - B. Accuse her of deliberately poisoning me, then give her the antidote
  - C. Throw the antidote away and stay with her, both of us poisoned and cut off from the world
  - D. Leave Padua without seeing her and drink the antidote alone
- **Author's path:** He curses her bitterly, then gives her the antidote. It kills her, and her last words are that there was more poison in his nature than in hers.
- **Axis:** Trust/love vs self-protection

## A03. The Birth-Mark — Nathaniel Hawthorne (1843)
- ~6500 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/512
- **Plot:** A scientist becomes obsessed with removing the small hand-shaped birthmark on his beautiful wife's cheek.
- **Focal:** Aylmer
- **Stop:** Georgiana has read his failed-experiment journal and says she will drink the potion whatever the risk. Stop before he gives it to her.
- **Question:** "My wife consents to an untested potion to remove the one flaw I can't stop seeing. My earlier experiments failed. What do I do?"
  - A. Give her the potion as planned
  - B. Refuse, and work to accept the mark
  - C. Keep testing it on other subjects first, delaying for months or years
  - D. Tell her the obsession is my failing, not her flaw, and destroy the potion
- **Author's path:** He gives her the potion. The mark fades, and she dies.
- **Axis:** Ambition/perfectionism vs a loved one's safety

## A04. The Minister's Black Veil — Nathaniel Hawthorne (1836)
- ~4500 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13707
- **Plot:** Parson Hooper starts wearing a black veil over his face and will not remove it. The veil cuts him off from his congregation and from his fiancée.
- **Focal:** Rev. Mr. Hooper
- **Stop:** Elizabeth, his fiancée, begs him to lift the veil just once, for her, and says she will leave him if he refuses. Stop before he answers.
- **Question:** "The woman I'm going to marry asks to see my face once, privately, or she will leave me. The veil is my vow and my sermon. What do I do?"
  - A. Keep the veil on and lose her
  - B. Lift it for her alone in private, then put it back on in public
  - C. Give the veil up entirely and explain what it symbolizes
  - D. Keep it on but explain what it means to her completely, hoping she stays
- **Author's path:** He refuses. She leaves, and he wears the veil until he dies.
- **Axis:** Integrity of a vow vs love and human connection

## A05. The Cask of Amontillado — Edgar Allan Poe (1846)
- ~2300 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2148
- **Plot:** Montresor lures Fortunato, who has insulted him, into his family catacombs during carnival, promising him a rare wine.
- **Focal:** Montresor
- **Stop:** Fortunato is chained in the niche, sober now and pleading 'For the love of God, Montresor!' Montresor has only the last tier of stones left to set. Stop before he finishes the wall.
- **Question:** "My enemy is chained in the niche, begging me. I have one tier of stones left. What do I do?"
  - A. Finish the wall
  - B. Free him and let the terror be his punishment
  - C. Leave him chained and walled in but tell others where he is, so he is found
  - D. Free him and confront him openly over the insults
- **Author's path:** He answers 'Yes, for the love of God,' sets the last stone, and is never found out.
- **Axis:** Revenge/honor vs mercy

## A06. The Tell-Tale Heart — Edgar Allan Poe (1843)
- ~2200 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2148
- **Plot:** The narrator kills the old man he lives with because of his 'vulture eye' and hides the body under the floorboards. Police then come to the house.
- **Focal:** The narrator
- **Stop:** The officers are sitting chatting directly over the floorboards, and the narrator thinks he hears the heart beating louder. Stop before he speaks.
- **Question:** "The police are sitting calmly above the body. They suspect nothing. I am in agony. What do I do?"
  - A. Keep up the act until they leave
  - B. Confess everything
  - C. Make an excuse to get them out of the house quickly
  - D. Admit there was 'a disturbance' and give a partial, false account
- **Author's path:** He shrieks, confesses, and tears up the planks.
- **Axis:** Self-preservation vs conscience/truth

## A07. The Gift of the Magi — O. Henry (1905)
- ~2100 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2776
- **Plot:** Della has $1.87 the day before Christmas and wants to buy her husband Jim a fob chain for his gold watch.
- **Focal:** Della Young
- **Stop:** Della stands at Madame Sofronie's hair shop, her hair let down. Stop before she sells it.
- **Question:** "I have $1.87 and want to give Jim a real gift. My hair is my only treasure and it would sell for $20. What do I do?"
  - A. Sell my hair and buy the fob chain
  - B. Keep my hair and buy a small gift with the $1.87
  - C. Tell Jim honestly that we can't afford gifts this year
  - D. Pawn or sell something else of the household's instead
- **Author's path:** She sells her hair and buys the chain. Jim has sold his watch to buy her combs.
- **Axis:** Self-sacrifice vs self-regard/prudence

## A08. A Retrieved Reformation — O. Henry (1903)
- ~3200 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1646
- **Plot:** Safecracker Jimmy Valentine goes straight under a new name, Ralph Spencer, and becomes engaged to a banker's daughter. A detective, Ben Price, tracks him down.
- **Focal:** Jimmy Valentine (as Ralph Spencer)
- **Stop:** A child is locked inside the new bank vault and will suffocate. Jimmy has his burglar's tools, and he does not yet know that Ben Price is watching. Stop before he acts.
- **Question:** "A little girl is suffocating in the locked vault. Opening it with my old tools will expose me in front of my fiancée and her family and wreck my new life. What do I do?"
  - A. Open the vault, accepting exposure
  - B. Urge them to send for a locksmith or blast the door, and keep my secret
  - C. Open it and then run away before anyone can question me
  - D. Open it in a way that makes it look accidental or easy, and hide my skill
- **Author's path:** He opens the vault in front of everyone and turns himself over to Price. Price pretends not to know him.
- **Axis:** Saving others vs self-preservation/new identity

## A09. The Last Leaf — O. Henry (1907)
- ~2900 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3707
- **Plot:** In Greenwich Village a young painter, Johnsy, is dying of pneumonia and believes she will die when the last ivy leaf falls. Old Behrman is a failed painter who lives downstairs.
- **Focal:** Behrman
- **Stop:** On the stormy night before the last leaf will fall, Behrman knows that painting a leaf on the wall outside means a night in freezing rain at his age. Stop before he goes out.
- **Question:** "A young girl downstairs has decided she will die when the last leaf falls. The storm will take it tonight. I could paint one, out there, in the freezing rain. What do I do?"
  - A. Paint the leaf on the wall in the storm
  - B. Go up and argue her out of the superstition
  - C. Tie or fix the real leaf so it stays, at less risk to myself
  - D. Do nothing. It is a foolish fancy, and the doctor will handle it
- **Author's path:** He paints the leaf in the storm. Johnsy recovers, and Behrman dies of pneumonia.
- **Axis:** Self-sacrifice vs self-preservation

## A10. The Man Who Would Be King — Rudyard Kipling (1888)
- ~12000 words · https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/8147
- **Plot:** Two British adventurers, Dravot and Carnehan, become 'kings' of Kafiristan by passing themselves off as gods. They have signed a contract forbidding women and drink.
- **Focal:** Daniel Dravot
- **Stop:** Dravot has decided to take a local wife, against the contract and the priests' fear, and Carnehan objects. Stop before he insists on the marriage.
- **Question:** "I rule these people as a god. I want a queen to found a dynasty, but my partner says it breaks our contract and the priests are frightened. What do I do?"
  - A. Insist on the marriage
  - B. Give up the idea and keep the contract
  - C. Hand power over to the local chiefs and leave with our wealth while we can
  - D. Admit to the chiefs that we are men, not gods, and rule openly on that basis
- **Author's path:** He insists. The terrified bride bites him, they see he bleeds, and the people kill him and crucify Carnehan.
- **Axis:** Loyalty to a pact vs personal desire/ambition

## A11. Lord Arthur Savile's Crime — Oscar Wilde (1887)

- **Focal:** Lord Arthur Savile
- **Stop:** Before his wedding, Lord Arthur has just been told the prophecy. Stop before he decides on the murder.
- **Question:** "I've been told I will commit a murder. I love Sybil and don't want her to marry a man with this hanging over him. What do I do?"
  - A. Do my 'duty': choose a victim and kill them before the wedding
  - B. Ignore the prophecy and marry
  - C. Tell Sybil and let her decide
  - D. Break off the engagement and live alone so I can't hurt her
- **Author's path:** After two failed attempts, he drowns the palmist himself and marries Sybil happily.
- **Axis:** Duty to a loved one vs the prohibition on killing (satire)

## A13. The Nightingale and the Rose — Oscar Wilde (1888)

## A14. To Build a Fire — Jack London (1908)

- **Question:** "My fire is out and my hands are dead. The dog could save me if I kill it and warm my hands in its body. What do I do?"
